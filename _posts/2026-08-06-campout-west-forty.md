This one was different from our usual trips, and I mean that in the best
way. No dogs, no quiet site in the woods, no two of us and a fire. Instead:
about forty LGBTQIA+ folks all camping in the same place for a weekend, an
organized schedule, and a campsite decorating contest. Jayson and I drove
up to Gilbert on Thursday for Northern Minnesota's CampOUT weekend at
[West Forty RV Park & Campground](https://westfortyrvpark.com/), timed to
line up with [Range Iron Pride](https://rangeironpride.org/) over in
Virginia.

As always, the shorter, in-the-moment version — photos, quick takes —
lives on Mastodon at **[@w0ger@woof.group](https://woof.group/@w0ger)**.

Short version: we booked next year before we left.

We were on site A3 — a small camper/tent site with 30-amp electric and
water, tucked into the wooded section of the campground. West Forty sits on
40 acres on the eastern face of the Laurentian Divide, and the A section
has real tree cover, which turned out to matter a lot more than I expected.
More on that below.

The weather was as close to perfect as August gets up here. Warm days, cool
nights, and the only rain we saw was early Friday morning — the kind that's
already done by the time you're properly awake. After a summer of watching
fire restrictions and smoke forecasts across northern Minnesota, getting a
clean, mild weekend on the Range felt like winning something.

The event itself is run by Paul and Chuck, and they've clearly done this
before. Thursday was check-in and decorating with the pavilion open for
whoever wanted to wander up. Friday had campground games in the evening —
cornhole, ax toss, bocce — then a group social and campfire that ran until
midnight, with s'mores provided. Saturday was the CookOUT: burgers, hot
dogs, all the fixings, and a potluck, followed by a social and sing-along
with Diddy Wish. Sunday morning, North Haven Coffee parked their truck by
the restroom building for everyone packing up, which is a genuinely
thoughtful touch when you've got a griddle to scrape and a tent to fold.

Both evening gatherings were the best part of the weekend, and I want to be
specific about why: it's a rare thing to be at a campground where you don't
have to do any calculating. No reading the room, no wondering. Just forty
people around a fire.

The theme was "Just Be Proud!" and judging happened Friday at 9 p.m., just
after dusk. That detail is the entire contest, and I don't think everyone
caught it.

Sunset Friday was 8:33. Full dark around 9:10. Which means anything you
decorate with that isn't lit is effectively invisible when the judges walk
by. Flags, banners, bunting — all of it disappears. So we planned around
light.

The centerpiece was an [Enbrighten Vibe](https://enbrightenme.com/) 48-foot
string with 24 RGBWIC bulbs. RGBWIC means every bulb is individually
addressable, so instead of picking one color, we mapped a full Pride
gradient across the whole string — four bulbs per color, red through
violet, and set it as a static scene rather than an animation. That was
deliberate. Judges get maybe fifteen seconds at your site, and they'll
never see a full animation cycle. A chase reads as generic party lights. A
static gradient reads as a flag.

We hung it as a single arc across the front of the site, broadside to the
loop road, about eight feet up with a gentle sag — tree to tree, using the
canopy the A section gave us. Throw a paracord line over the branch first,
then haul the string up to it. Ten minutes if you do it in that order,
considerably longer if you don't.

Under the arc: our Pride flag with a few of the string bulbs nearest it set
to white (colored light on a rainbow flag flattens the stripes — white
keeps them separate), a "Don't Stop Until You're Proud" neon sign, and a
rainbow camper picnic table cover with matching bench covers.

**Second place.** A $50 certificate toward our next stay, which we have
already applied to next year's reservation.

And the honest gear lesson: this worked because of shore power. A 30-amp
pedestal meant we could run real plug-in lighting from dusk to midnight
without thinking about it, while a lot of sites were working with
battery-powered options. If you take one thing from this post, it's that
the hanging color lights did the heavy lifting — height and sequence beat
quantity.

## What's Working Well

**Enbrighten Vibe RGBWIC string lights.** The star of the weekend.
Individually addressable bulbs meant we could build the gradient exactly,
save it as a scene, and switch to it at 8:45 with one tap. One note for
anyone buying these: they pair over 2.4GHz Wi-Fi, which you will not have
at a campground. Pair them and save your scenes at home *before* you leave.
Once configured, Bluetooth control works fine on site.

**The tree cover on the A sites.** Shade during setup on a warm afternoon,
and canopy to hang lights from at night. I'd book in that section again on
those grounds alone.

**West Forty's shower building.** New, and genuinely one of the cleanest
campground bathrooms we've used. Not a small thing over three days.

**Being able to leave the roof top tent up.** We deliberately planned a
weekend where the truck never had to move after Thursday, and that turned
out to be the right call. Once the iKamper is deployed, moving the truck
means striking camp, and there's nothing worse than doing that twice for a
lunch run.

## What Could Be Better

**The TT-30 to 5-15 adapter didn't work with the Rivian charger.** I bought
a 12" RVGUARD dogbone specifically so the 30-amp pedestal could run our
fridge and lights, and for that it was fine. But I also tried it with the
Rivian portable charger and could not get it to charge. Returned it to
Amazon. Worth knowing that West Forty has dedicated EV charging on-site, so
this was a convenience thing rather than a problem — Arden Hills to Gilbert
and back is well inside range with buffer. But if you were counting on
charging off your site pedestal, don't.

**Porketta plans, foiled.** I had a whole Thursday-dinner plan built around
picking up hot porketta from a deli counter on the drive in — it's the Iron
Range's signature meat and it seemed like the correct thing to eat on our
first night in Gilbert. Couldn't find any pre-cooked anywhere. We grabbed
pulled pork instead, which was perfectly good, but I'm still thinking about
the porketta.

**We over-ate at lunch and it rearranged dinner.** Not a complaint. See
below.

## Around the Range

Friday we spent the day exploring Gilbert, and lunch at
[Fitz's Wandering Pines Eatery & Pub](https://www.facebook.com/fitzswanderingpines/)
on Broadway was the highlight. Knotty pine interior, small-town-diner feel
with a little attached bar, friendly service. Go there.

We also had a full slate of options we didn't use — Lake Ore-Be-Gone is
1.5 miles from the campground with a swim beach and famously clear water
(it's a flooded mine pit), the Mesabi Trail runs right past the campground,
and the Iron Range OHV Park is at the edge of town. Gilbert bills itself as
the off-road capital of northern Minnesota and West Forty is set up for it,
right down to the ATV wash station and staging area. Next year.

## The Menu

Some of this went to plan and some of it didn't, which is camping.

**Thursday**

- *Dinner* — Pulled pork sandwiches on the Blackstone. The porketta backup
  plan, and it did the job.

**Friday**

- *Breakfast* — Bacon, eggs, and hash browns on the griddle. French press
  coffee.
- *Lunch* — Fitz's Wandering Pines in Gilbert. Excellent, and large.
- *Dinner* — Kept light, because of the above.

**Saturday**

- *Breakfast* — Breakfast burritos on the griddle.
- *Lunch* — Ribeye fajitas. These were supposed to be Friday's dinner, but
  we were still full from Fitz's, so they got moved. Rubbed the ribeye with
  cumin, garlic powder, and chili powder, zoned the griddle with the
  peppers and onions on medium and the sear on high, pulled at 125°F,
  rested ten minutes, sliced against the grain, and finished with lime and
  the resting juices poured back over. Worth the schedule shuffle.
- *Dinner* — The CookOUT at the pavilion. Burgers, hot dogs, and everyone's
  potluck contributions.

**Sunday**

- *Breakfast* — North Haven Coffee from the truck, and leftovers off the
  griddle while we packed.

The Blackstone handled every meal this trip. The Coleman and the cast iron
stayed home, and I didn't miss them once.

Usually I close these by talking about who was along and who wasn't, and
this time the answer is complicated in a good way. Echo and Dee OhGee
stayed home, and I did miss them — no huskies at the campfire, no two sets
of ears going up every time somebody walked past the site. But this wasn't
really a dog weekend. It was a people weekend.

Jayson and I met a bunch of new folks over those few days, and we're hoping
to see some of them at other camping events. That's the thing about an
organized weekend like this: you show up for the campground and the
schedule, and what you leave with is a list of people you want to camp near
again.

We booked A3 for next year before we left.
